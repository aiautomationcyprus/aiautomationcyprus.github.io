Meta is testing a new AI platform called Project Hatch, and it is unlike anything the company has shipped before. Hatch is not a chatbot. It is not a content recommendation algorithm. It is a persistent AI agent that lives on your phone or desktop, remembers your preferences across sessions, and can take actions in the real world on your behalf -- booking services, managing purchases, arranging childcare, and handling recurring tasks without being asked twice.

The implications for businesses are serious, and Cyprus-based companies should pay attention.

## What Hatch Actually Does

Project Hatch is being tested internally at Meta as a standalone app on iOS, Android, and the web. It combines several capabilities that have existed separately across other tools but never been unified at this scale.

**Persistent memory.** Unlike most AI assistants that forget you the moment a session ends, Hatch maintains context across days and weeks. If you tell it you need a caterer for an event in three weeks, it will remember, follow up, and track progress without a reminder from you.

**Long-running goals.** Hatch supports what Meta calls "goals" -- multi-step tasks that unfold over time. You define the objective, and the agent works toward it in the background, checking back in when it needs input or when something changes.

**Connectors.** Hatch integrates directly with email, calendar services, Spotify, Instagram, and third-party services. This is not a copy-paste integration. The agent can read your inbox, identify relevant threads, draft replies, and reschedule meetings -- all from a single interface.

**Browser and computer use.** Hatch can operate a browser autonomously. Internal testers have used it to research purchases, fill out forms, and complete transactions. This is the part that has generated the most discussion: an AI that can actually buy things, not just recommend them.

**Spaces.** For users managing multiple projects or contexts -- a freelancer handling three clients, a business owner separating personal and company tasks -- Hatch organizes work into separate Spaces, keeping results, files, and progress isolated by project.

## Who Is Behind It

This is worth noting: Hatch is currently powered by Anthropic's Claude during its development phase. Meta plans to transition it to Muse Spark, its own proprietary model, at launch. The fact that Meta chose Claude as the development backbone speaks to where the performance benchmarks currently sit -- and it explains why the agent behavior in early tests has been described as unusually capable for a Meta product.

Meta is not alone in this race. Google is running a parallel project called Remy, targeting similar capabilities around persistent personal agents. OpenAI's memory features and operator integrations are moving in the same direction. But Hatch is notable for how aggressively it combines all of these elements into a consumer-facing superapp, rather than a developer API or enterprise tool.

## What This Costs

Official pricing has not been confirmed. Industry reporting suggests subscription tiers, with premium plans potentially reaching $200 per month for full access to long-running goals, connectors, and computer use. That figure is in line with what OpenAI charges for its most capable plans and what enterprise customers currently pay for agentic automation tools.

If that pricing holds, Hatch enters the market as a prosumer and SMB product, not a mass-market free tool. For businesses that currently spend on virtual assistants, scheduling software, or basic automation workflows, that cost structure will be familiar.

## What It Means for Businesses in Cyprus

For local businesses -- hospitality operators, real estate agencies, legal and accounting firms, retail -- the practical question is not whether this technology is impressive. It clearly is. The question is how quickly tools like Hatch reshape what customers and employees expect from automation.

Consider the purchase flow. If a customer's AI agent can research, compare, and complete a purchase autonomously, the businesses that get selected will be the ones whose digital presence is structured for agent interaction: clear pricing, machine-readable availability, fast response to queries. Businesses that rely on a back-and-forth WhatsApp conversation to close a sale will find that model increasingly bypassed.

On the internal side, a persistent agent that manages calendars, drafts emails, tracks supplier conversations, and handles recurring admin tasks is exactly what a two-person SMB currently does manually, or pays a part-time coordinator to handle. At $200 a month, Hatch would cost less than four hours of outsourced admin work.

The disruption here is not dramatic and sudden. It is the slow compression of tasks that used to require a human. Scheduling. Follow-ups. Research. Purchase completion. Each one individually small. Together, they represent a significant share of operational overhead for most small businesses.

## The Timing

Hatch has no confirmed launch date. Meta is running internal tests now, with a consumer rollout described as near-term. Given the competitive pressure from Google Remy and OpenAI's operator ecosystem, the timeline is likely measured in months, not years.

This is the moment to review which parts of your business operations could be handed to an agent -- and which parts genuinely require human judgment. The companies that do that review now, before the tools ship broadly, will be the ones positioned to adopt them fast rather than scrambling to catch up.

Persistent AI agents are no longer a research concept. They are being tested at scale by the largest technology company in the world. That changes the conversation.
